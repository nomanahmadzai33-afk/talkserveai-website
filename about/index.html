Our Story

Built by someone who's worked the floor

TalkServe AI wasn't built in a boardroom. It was built by Noman Ahmadzai, a waiter working the floor at a restaurant in La Moraleja, Alcobendas, in Madrid.

Working service means watching the same problem happen night after night: the phone rings during the dinner rush, every hand is full, and nobody can get to it. The call goes to voicemail — or nowhere. That's a reservation gone, sometimes a regular who doesn't call back. It's not a hypothetical for Noman — it's what he sees from the floor, shift after shift.

"I wasn't trying to build a tech company. I was trying to fix the thing I watched happen at work every week."

So outside of shifts, nights and days off, Noman started building. Not a call center, not a script-reading bot — an AI receptionist that actually picks up, in the languages the restaurant's own customers speak, and hands off to a human the moment it matters. TalkServe AI grew out of that: a hands-on understanding of what a restaurant actually needs at the host stand and on the phone, not a generic enterprise pitch.

That's still how it's run today — directly, by someone who still clocks in for restaurant shifts and knows the difference between a feature that sounds good in a demo and one that actually holds up on a Friday night.
